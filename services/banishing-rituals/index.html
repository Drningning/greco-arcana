Banishing Rituals for Homes & Businesses

— Clear the territory before establishing your own.

A banishing ritual clears a space of accumulated influence — psychic, emotional, and energetic residue that builds up in any place humans have lived, worked, or struggled. Performed on-site throughout New Jersey and the greater New York region.

When to Consider a Banishing

  • Before moving into a new home or rental. Clear the imprint of previous occupants before establishing your own.
  • After a separation, illness, or death in the home. Reset the space when the major event has passed.
  • Before closing on a real estate purchase or sale. Joseph has been engaged for this work by realtors, buyers, and investors throughout New Jersey.
  • For office spaces, particularly after a difficult tenancy. Commercial spaces accumulate influence as readily as residential ones.
  • When the territory simply feels off. If you have read this far, you already know what that means.

What the Working Includes

  • Initial consultation to understand the space and its history
  • Full Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram performed on-site
  • Ancillary work appropriate to the territory — additional pentagrams, hexagrams, water and incense, salt, and other implements as the case requires
  • Consecration of the space at the conclusion of the banishing
  • Written notes on follow-up care of the space, where appropriate

Service Area

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a banishing ritual, and what does it actually accomplish?
A banishing ritual is a ceremonial act of clearing, ordering, and re-establishing a spiritual boundary. Just as a physical space can become dirty, cluttered, or stale, a spiritual atmosphere can become heavy, confused, agitated, or disordered. A banishing clears that atmosphere and restores the space to a cleaner, more harmonious condition. This isn't about creating fear; it's about restoring order. A proper banishing removes unwanted influences, cuts through stagnant energy, quiets spiritual noise, and reclaims the space — and then sets a boundary, so the place is not merely "cleared" but properly set. The goal is not drama. The goal is peace.
What does the work involve?
The work is built around a full ceremonial banishing, including the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram, with ancillary work proper to the territory. Without giving away everything I reserve for the work itself: I don't treat every space as the same. A home is not an office; a business is not a newly purchased property; a healing room is not a retail floor. Each has its own function, atmosphere, history, and spiritual texture. The core ritual establishes the ceremonial structure; the ancillary work adapts the operation to the actual space in front of me. Depending on the situation, that may include prayer, psalmic or angelic work, elemental balancing, threshold work, incense, salt, water, candle work, and planetary or directional correspondences. The ritual has a structure, but the space is treated as a living place, not a generic room with walls.
Do you perform banishing rituals for homes and businesses?
Yes — for homes, apartments, offices, commercial spaces, studios, treatment rooms, and businesses. A residence is approached with attention to peace, protection, rest, family life, and the relationship between the people living there and the space itself; the goal is usually harmony and safety. A business or office is approached with attention to order, productivity, clean communication, client flow, and professional atmosphere; the goal is usually clarity, flow, protection, and proper function. Both carry a spiritual atmosphere, and both benefit from being properly cleared and set.
Can this be done before moving into a new home or office?
Yes — it's one of the best times to do it. A clearing can be performed before moving into a new home, opening a new office, or occupying a property that has carried difficult history: stress, conflict, grief, vacancy, or illness. For real estate clients, it can also be done before or around a closing. A property doesn't only change hands legally; it changes stewardship. The practical side handles title, keys, contracts, and inspections — this work addresses the spiritual handoff, so the new owner enters cleanly and intentionally, rather than simply inheriting whatever came before them.
What are the signs that a space may need clearing?
People usually call when a space feels "off" and ordinary explanations aren't enough. Common signs include heaviness, tension, poor sleep, repeated conflict, a sense of being watched or crowded, strange emotional shifts on entering, persistent obstruction, uncomfortable dreams, children or animals reacting strangely, or rooms that feel colder and more oppressive than others. Businesses may call when the office feels stagnant, communication keeps breaking down, clients seem uneasy, or the space never holds momentum. Sometimes the cause is obvious — a difficult prior owner, divorce, death, illness, bankruptcy. Other times there's no dramatic story; the space just needs a reset. Not every uncomfortable feeling is spiritual — sometimes a house needs sunlight, fresh air, a contractor, or a therapist — but when the atmosphere itself feels persistently disordered, a clearing may be appropriate.
Is this religious?
The work is spiritual and ceremonial, but it doesn't require you to share my exact beliefs. My practice comes from the Western hermetic and ceremonial magic tradition, and uses prayer, sacred names, symbolic structure, and angelic and elemental correspondences from that tradition. You don't need to be an occultist, a Mason, a magician, or even especially religious for the work to be meaningful. What matters is respect. If you're open to the idea that a space can carry atmosphere, memory, or symbolic weight, you already understand the basic principle — you don't need the technical language; that's my job. I do ask that clients treat the work sincerely. This is not entertainment, and it is not a Halloween attraction with better incense.
Do you travel for on-site work, and what does the visit look like?
Yes — I travel for on-site banishings and house clearings throughout New Jersey and the surrounding region. Distance depends on location, the nature of the work, and scheduling, and travel fees may apply depending on how far the site is. The visit usually begins with a brief walkthrough and conversation: what's been happening, which areas feel most affected, and what you want the space to become. Then I perform the ritual work. Most residential clearings take roughly 60 to 90 minutes; larger homes, commercial spaces, or more complicated situations may take longer. You don't need to memorize anything or participate in a complex way — mostly you need to be present, respectful, and available if I need access to specific rooms. Beforehand, I ask that the space be accessible, reasonably clean, and quiet enough for ritual work. Spiritual work isn't a substitute for basic order: if the room looks like a raccoon got a law degree and started filing paperwork, we have to deal with that too.
Who needs to be present?
For a home, it's best for the owner or primary resident to be present; for a business, the owner, manager, or person responsible for the space. Other family members or employees don't always need to attend, but it helps when the main people connected to the space are aware and supportive. A clearing is strongest when the person responsible consciously agrees to the intention: this place is being cleared, ordered, protected, and restored to right use.
Is the effect permanent, and do you offer follow-up?
A banishing can create a very strong reset, but no clearing should be treated as a one-time magic eraser for every future problem. Spaces are living environments; they absorb emotion, conflict, stress, and the habits of the people who occupy them, and if the same patterns continue unchecked, the atmosphere can grow heavy again. Afterward I may recommend simple upkeep — prayer, incense, salt, candle work, regular cleaning, threshold practices, or a periodic reset. For most homes and businesses, one serious clearing establishes a new baseline; spaces with heavy use or a complicated history may need occasional follow-up. I do offer follow-up when needed, and may check in to see how the space feels. The goal is not to make you dependent on me — it's to restore the space, give you clarity, and help you maintain peace and order going forward.
