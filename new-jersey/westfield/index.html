The Strange Imagination of Charles Addams

Behind Westfield's handsome homes, tree-lined streets, and traditional downtown lies a wonderfully peculiar connection to the macabre.

Westfield was the hometown of Charles Addams, the celebrated cartoonist whose strange and endearing creations became the Addams Family.

Addams transformed the ordinary American household into something beautifully unsettling.

His characters delighted in gloomy mansions, unusual experiments, graveyards, supernatural imagery, and an inversion of almost everything their respectable neighbors considered normal.

Yet beneath the darkness, the Addams Family was unusually affectionate, loyal, and comfortable with its own eccentricities.

It was a remarkably clever way to turn conventional expectations upside down.

Westfield still celebrates its famous son through AddamsFest, a community festival devoted to his legacy and the town's darker sense of humor.

But Westfield's interest in ghostly things doesn't end with cartoons.

The Ghosts of the Reeve House

The historic Reeve House dates to 1872 and serves as a home for the Westfield Historical Society.

During AddamsFest, the society has hosted paranormal investigations of the building, inviting visitors to explore reports of unusual activity and consider whether former residents might still be present.

The house witnessed more than a century of family history before becoming a museum.

Whether anything supernatural remains there is an open question, but the building has become an intriguing meeting place for local history and paranormal curiosity.
