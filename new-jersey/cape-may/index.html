The Woman Who Never Left the House

In Cape May stands a magnificent Victorian mansion built in 1879 for Dr. Emlen Physick Jr.

With its distinctive architecture, broad porches, and carefully restored interiors, the Emlen Physick Estate is one of New Jersey's great historic houses.

It's also one of the state's best-known reputed hauntings.

According to accounts collected by psychic medium and author Craig McManus, several members of the Physick household may have remained attached to their former home.

One is Dr. Physick's Aunt Emilie.

McManus described her as an energetic presence who continued to keep watch over the estate, even during the years when the building fell into disrepair.

Another reported presence is Frances Ralston, Dr. Physick's mother, whose bedroom has become a focus of the home's paranormal stories.

The accounts even include the ghosts of Dr. Physick's dogs.

That last detail is particularly charming, especially considering the household's reported disagreements over whether the dogs should be allowed indoors.

The estate's caretakers and paranormal investigators have shared numerous accounts of unexplained sounds, impressions, and reported apparitions.

Whether the experiences represent supernatural phenomena is a question visitors may answer for themselves.

But there's a reason Cape May's ghost stories are so closely connected to its Victorian past.

When Speaking with the Dead Became Fashionable

The nineteenth century witnessed an extraordinary revival of interest in Spiritualism.

Across America and Europe, people gathered for séances, experimented with spirit communication, consulted mediums, and explored ideas about life after death.

Victorian households were often fascinated by the possibility that departed loved ones could communicate with the living.

The period produced an unusual intersection of religious inquiry, scientific investigation, popular entertainment, and genuine spiritual conviction.

Cape May's preserved Victorian architecture provides a remarkable setting in which to explore that history.

The organization responsible for preserving the Physick Estate, Cape May MAC, even offers historical programs exploring Victorian Spiritualism and the methods people once used to attempt communication with spirits.

The nineteenth-century fascination with the unseen wasn't confined to forgotten books.

It was part of the culture of an entire era.
