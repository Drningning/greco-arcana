The Hessian Who Wouldn't Leave

Princeton has never lacked for great minds, ambitious students, or unusual stories.

But one of its more peculiar traditions concerns a young Hessian soldier who reportedly died during the Revolutionary War.

According to local folklore, the soldier's ghost began appearing in an old house on Edgehill Street, said to have sheltered him after the Battle of Princeton.

Witnesses described a youthful figure wearing an oversized military coat.

His appearances reportedly followed a curious pattern. Each Christmas Eve, the soldier would appear, smile, and disappear up the chimney.

In 1939, the Reverend Arthur Kinsolving reportedly attempted to exorcize the apparition using prayers from an old Church of England prayer book.

The attempt was apparently unsuccessful.

The family, however, seems to have been rather less troubled by their spectral resident than the clergyman.

They considered him an amiable presence.

That's an unusual conclusion to a ghost story, but a rather charming one.

Aaron Burr and the Spirits of Nassau Hall

Princeton's ghostly traditions also include one of America's most controversial political figures.

Aaron Burr, the former vice president who killed Alexander Hamilton in their famous duel, is buried in Princeton Cemetery.

For generations, students have repeated stories of Burr's apparition wandering the campus and cemetery.

An old student custom even involved visiting his grave on rainy autumn nights in hopes of seeing the former vice president emerge from the darkness.

Burr isn't Princeton's only reported spirit.

Its historic buildings have inspired tales of Revolutionary soldiers, former residents, and university figures who supposedly remain attached to familiar surroundings.

Princeton has become a place where education, history, and ghost stories occupy the same ancient halls.
