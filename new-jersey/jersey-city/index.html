The Eyes in the Church Tower

For generations, residents of Jersey City have shared a peculiar story about St. Joseph's Church on Pavonia Avenue.

On certain nights, two strange yellowish lights have reportedly appeared in the church's steeple, resembling a pair of eyes looking out over the surrounding neighborhood.

The legend became especially unsettling in 1921, when church sexton Matthew Guarino reportedly went to investigate the phenomenon. According to the story, he was later discovered dead in the church.

The mysterious eyes were reported again in 1954, when their appearance on Good Friday stirred considerable local interest. Attempts to discover an ordinary explanation reportedly included spreading flour in the tower to reveal whether anyone had entered.

The story survives today as one of Jersey City's strangest pieces of religious folklore.

Were the lights reflections, deliberate tricks, or something more unusual? The mystery has outlived the people who first described it.

The Lady in White at the Railroad Terminal

Across Jersey City, at the historic Central Railroad of New Jersey Terminal, another apparition has entered local legend.

The terminal once carried enormous numbers of travelers toward New York Harbor. Its waiting rooms witnessed reunions, departures, immigration, and the daily movement of working families.

But according to local ghost stories, one traveler never quite departed.

Visitors and employees have reportedly seen a woman in white moving through the terminal, sometimes disappearing before anyone can approach her.

Another legend concerns a runaway locomotive that supposedly moved without a driver.

The stories belong to the city's paranormal folklore. The terminal itself, however, is a very real monument to Jersey City's history.
