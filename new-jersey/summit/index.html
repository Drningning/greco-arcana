A Gathering of Witches at the Carter House

Summit is known for handsome residences, beautiful gardens, and a prosperous downtown. It isn't usually the first town that comes to mind when someone mentions witchcraft.

But the town has its own playful relationship with the mysterious.

At the historic Carter House, the Summit Historical Society hosts community events celebrating local history.

In October 2026, one such event was called Witches' Night Out.

Guests were invited to gather in their witches' hats for an evening supporting the preservation of Summit's historic museum.

The event wasn't evidence of some forgotten coven or an ancient magical order. It was a modern celebration with a sense of humor.

But it does reveal something appealing about the continuing fascination with witchcraft, folklore, and traditional symbols.

Even in communities that appear thoroughly modern, the old imagery retains its power.

The Clearing: A Garden with a Remarkable Past

Another of Summit's unusual historic places is Reeves-Reed Arboretum, once known as The Clearing.

The property began its transformation into a landscaped country estate during the late nineteenth century.

Over the decades, distinguished landscape designers shaped its gardens, paths, and plantings, creating a place devoted to the beauty and ordering of nature.

For a student of natural magic, the history of such a garden holds an entirely different fascination.

The Western esoteric tradition has long studied the symbolic associations of plants, seasons, planetary influences, and the relationship between nature and human intention.

There's no documented reason to describe The Clearing as an occult garden.

But its existence reminds us of the enduring human desire to understand nature, cultivate it, and discover meaning within its patterns.

That is familiar territory to the natural magician.
