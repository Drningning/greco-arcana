When the Old Buildings Begin to Talk

There's a particular kind of New Jersey town where the past never seems very far away.

Red Bank is one of them.

Beneath the lively restaurants, theaters, and fashionable shops is an older community with a history of maritime commerce, family businesses, changing fortunes, and buildings that have witnessed generations of human drama.

Some of those buildings have accumulated reputations for hauntings.

Among the best-known is the Dublin House, a distinctive historic establishment that appears on Red Bank ghost tours.

Local paranormal storytellers associate the property with reports of restless spirits and unusual occurrences.

Nearby sites, including the former First National Bank building and the Globe Hotel property, also figure in the town's ghost-tour traditions.

Today, visitors can walk through downtown Red Bank on the Requiem of the Reaper tour, where guides explore the area's darker stories, mysterious incidents, and legends.

The Value of an Old Ghost Story

One of the interesting things about such stories is how they survive.

Businesses change hands. Residents move away. Buildings are renovated. Streets take on new appearances.

But a story attached to a particular place can remain surprisingly persistent.

Whether every reported apparition has an ordinary explanation or something else lies behind a few of them, folklore preserves the emotional history of a community.

And those stories offer a good reminder that the visible world is only one part of human experience.
