Inner Council · selected author

Marsilio Ficino

1433–1499

Renaissance Platonism and Hermetic philosophy

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

A Florentine priest and physician working under the patronage of the Medici, Ficino translated the Hermetic treatises into Latin (printed 1471), the complete works of Plato (printed 1484) and the Enneads of Plotinus (printed 1492). His De vita libri tres appeared in 1489.

Contribution to the tradition

Ficino reintroduced Plato, Plotinus and Hermes to the Latin West and joined them in one philosophy. His astrological medicine in the third book of <cite>De vita</cite> is the bridge between Platonic cosmology and practical celestial magic.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced

Three Books on Life

Marsilio Ficino

Three Books on Life (De vita libri tres)

First published 1489; critical edition and translation by Carol V. Kaske and John R. Clark (1989)

Where to read
Concentrate on Book III, On Obtaining Life from the Heavens.
Why it belongs here
Ficino applies Platonic and Hermetic cosmology to the health of the scholar, using music, images, foods and timing to draw on celestial influence. This is the bridge from philosophy to practice.
Investigate while reading
How does Ficino explain the medium through which the heavens act on the body? Which of his practices are medical, which astrological and which magical?
Connections
Applies the Hermetic and Platonic texts he translated. Agrippa systematizes this approach; Warnock teaches its astrological reasoning in Stage IV.

Place in the tradition

Historical influences

  • Corpus Hermeticum Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino translated the Corpus Hermeticum into Latin for Cosimo de' Medici; it was printed in 1471. (Ficino, Pimander (1471))

  • Plato Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino translated the complete works of Plato into Latin, printed in 1484.

  • Plotinus Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino translated the Enneads into Latin, printed in 1492.

  • Iamblichus Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino made a Latin paraphrase of On the Mysteries, printed in 1497.

  • Picatrix Documented historical influence

    Ficino's De vita III draws on the astrological magic of the Picatrix tradition. (Ficino, De vita libri tres III)

  • Traditional astrology Documented historical influence

    Ficino's astrological medicine applies traditional astrology.

Subsequent developments

  • Renaissance occult philosophy Documented historical influence

    Ficino's translations and De vita are central to Renaissance occult philosophy.

  • Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa Documented historical influence

    Agrippa draws extensively on Ficino's De vita in the first two books of his Occult Philosophy. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia I–II)
