Outer Council · selected author

Plato

c. 428/427–348/347 BCE

Classical philosophy

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

An Athenian philosopher and student of Socrates, Plato founded the Academy in Athens. His dialogues include the Republic, the Phaedrus and the Timaeus.

Contribution to the tradition

Plato's theory of Forms and the cosmology of the <cite>Timaeus</cite>, in which a divine craftsman orders the world by number and proportion, are the deepest root of the Western idea that the visible world images an invisible order.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Intermediate · Public domain

Timaeus

Plato

Written c. 360 BCE; translated by Benjamin Jowett (1871)

Where to read
Read Timaeus's account of the making of the cosmos (from 27c). Give particular attention to the World Soul (34b–37c) and the regular solids assigned to the elements (53c–56c).
Why it belongs here
The Timaeus is the single most important source for the Western idea that the visible world is ordered by number, proportion and an invisible pattern.
Investigate while reading
What is the relationship between the eternal model and the visible world? Why does Plato build the elements out of triangles?
Connections
Supplies the philosophy behind the sacred architecture met in Stage I. Plotinus and Proclus systematize the metaphysics; Critchlow returns to the geometry.
