Inner Council · selected author

Plotinus

c. 204/205–270

Neoplatonic metaphysics

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

Plotinus studied philosophy in Alexandria under Ammonius Saccas and taught in Rome from 244. His treatises were edited after his death by his student Porphyry into six sets of nine, the Enneads.

Contribution to the tradition

Plotinus describes reality as proceeding from the One through Intellect and Soul and returning to its source. That pattern of procession and return underlies Renaissance Hermeticism, Christian mysticism and the initiatory ascent of the later mystery schools.

Essential reading · Advanced · Public domain

Enneads

Plotinus

The Enneads (selected treatises)

Composed 253–270; edited by Porphyry

Where to read
Selected treatises: I.6 (On Beauty), V.1 (On the Three Primary Hypostases) and VI.9 (On the Good, or the One).
Why it belongs here
Plotinus describes reality as flowing from the One through Intellect and Soul, and the soul's return to its source. That pattern of descent and ascent shapes everything that follows.
Investigate while reading
How can the One be the source of everything without being diminished? What does the soul have to do to return?
Connections
Interprets Plato. Iamblichus and Proclus develop this metaphysics in different directions; Ficino brings it to the Renaissance in Stage III.

Place in the tradition

Historical influences

  • Platonism Documented historical influence

    Plotinus understood himself as an interpreter of Plato. (Plotinus, Enneads)

Subsequent developments

  • Neoplatonism Documented historical influence

    Plotinus's Enneads are the founding texts of Neoplatonism. (Enneads, ed. Porphyry)

  • Iamblichus Documented historical influence

    Iamblichus studied in the circle of Porphyry, Plotinus's student and editor, and developed theurgy in debate with that school. (Iamblichus, On the Mysteries)

  • Marsilio Ficino Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino translated the Enneads into Latin, printed in 1492.
