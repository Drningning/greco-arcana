Aleister Crowley

1875–1947

Ceremonial magic and Thelema

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

An English occultist, poet and mountaineer, Crowley was initiated into the Golden Dawn in 1898. In 1904 he received The Book of the Law, the founding text of Thelema; he co-founded the A∴A∴ about 1907 and later led the Ordo Templi Orientis.

Contribution to the tradition

Crowley reworked the Golden Dawn's ceremonial synthesis into his own system and wrote one of the most thorough modern treatments of magical theory and practice. He is studied here for that technical contribution, with the critical distance any serious student applies to a controversial figure.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced

Magick: Liber ABA, Book 4

Aleister Crowley

Magick: Liber ABA, Book 4, Parts I–IV

Composed 1912–1930s; collected edition edited by Hymenaeus Beta (Weiser, 1994; 2nd ed. 1997)

Where to read
Part I (Mysticism) and Part II (Magick: Elementary Theory) first; Part III (Magick in Theory and Practice) later.
Why it belongs here
One of the most thorough modern treatments of magical theory and the training of concentration, written by a former member of the Golden Dawn.
Investigate while reading
Compare Crowley's account of the magical weapons with the Golden Dawn's. Which changes are technical and which reflect his religious system?
