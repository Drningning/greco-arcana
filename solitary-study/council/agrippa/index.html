Inner Council · selected author

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa

1486–1535

Renaissance occult philosophy

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

A German physician, jurist and scholar, Agrippa completed an early draft of De occulta philosophia about 1510 and published the full three books in 1533. He also wrote the skeptical De incertitudine et vanitate scientiarum (1530).

Contribution to the tradition

Agrippa organized the magical knowledge of antiquity and the Middle Ages into three worlds: elemental, celestial and intellectual. His synthesis joins natural magic, astrology, Christian Cabala and ceremonial magic, and almost every later Western system draws on his tables.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced · Public domain

Three Books of Occult Philosophy

Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa

Three Books of Occult Philosophy (De occulta philosophia libri tres)

Draft c. 1510; complete Latin edition 1533; English translation by J. F. 1651

Where to read
Book I (natural magic and the elemental world), Book II (mathematics and the celestial world) and Book III (ceremonial magic and the intellectual world). Books II and III are also at esotericarchives.com.
Why it belongs here
Agrippa is the central junction of this curriculum. His three worlds organize natural magic, astrology, Christian Cabala and ceremonial magic into one structure that later systems inherit.
Investigate while reading
Map each of the three books to the world it describes. How does a power descend from the intellectual world into a stone or a plant?
Connections
Draws on Ficino, Pico, Reuchlin and Albertus. Every later stage returns to Agrippa: Stage IV to Book I, Stages V and VII to Books II and III.

Place in the tradition

Historical influences

  • Marsilio Ficino Documented historical influence

    Agrippa draws extensively on Ficino's De vita in the first two books of his Occult Philosophy. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia I–II)

  • Christian Cabala Documented historical influence

    Agrippa lectured on Reuchlin's De verbo mirifico at Dole in 1509 and drew Christian Cabala into his third book. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia III)

  • Albertus Magnus Documented historical influence

    Agrippa cites Albertus on the virtues of natural things. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia I)

Subsequent developments

  • The three worlds Documented historical influence

    Agrippa arranges occult philosophy into elemental, celestial and intellectual worlds. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia (1533))

  • Natural magic Documented historical influence

    Book I of Agrippa's Occult Philosophy is devoted to natural magic.

  • John Dee Documented historical influence

    Dee owned and studied Agrippa's Occult Philosophy.

  • The Golden Dawn Textual transmission or adaptation

    Golden Dawn teaching draws on Agrippa's tables of correspondence.
