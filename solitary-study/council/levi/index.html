Inner Council · selected author

Éliphas Lévi

1810–1875

Occult philosophy, Qabalah and the Tarot

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

Born Alphonse-Louis Constant in Paris, Lévi trained for the Catholic priesthood before leaving the seminary and turning to occult philosophy. His Dogme et rituel de la haute magie (1854–1856) was followed by Histoire de la magie (1860) and La Clef des grands mystères (1861).

Contribution to the tradition

Lévi is the hinge between Renaissance occult philosophy and the modern occult revival. He presented magic as a coherent philosophy, systematized the doctrine of the Astral Light, and connected the twenty-two Tarot trumps with the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet. That connection, revised by the Golden Dawn, is the foundation of Hermetic Tarot.

Transcendental Magic

Éliphas Lévi

Transcendental Magic: Its Doctrine and Ritual (Dogme et rituel de la haute magie)

French original 1854–1856; English translation by A. E. Waite 1896

Where to read
Part I (the Doctrine) and Part II (the Ritual), each in twenty-two chapters keyed to the trumps of the Tarot. In Stage VIII, return to the chapters on the Tarot.
Why it belongs here
Lévi joined Qabalah, ceremonial magic and the Tarot into a single philosophy, and the modern occult revival begins with him.
Investigate while reading
How does Lévi use the Hebrew letters to organize his book? What does he mean by the Astral Light?
Connections
The Golden Dawn revised Lévi's attributions of the trumps to the letters (Stage VIII).

Place in the tradition

Subsequent developments

  • Hermetic Qabalah Documented historical influence

    Lévi made the Kabbalah the backbone of a modern philosophy of magic. (Lévi, Dogme et rituel (1854–56))

  • Helena P. Blavatsky Documented historical influence

    Blavatsky cites Lévi in Isis Unveiled. (Blavatsky, Isis Unveiled (1877))

  • Albert Pike Textual transmission or adaptation

    Substantial portions of Morals and Dogma draw on Lévi's writings. (Pike, Morals and Dogma)

  • The Golden Dawn Documented historical influence

    The Golden Dawn developed its attributions of the Tarot to the Hebrew letters from Lévi, with revisions.

  • Hermetic Tarot Documented historical influence

    Lévi joined the twenty-two trumps to the twenty-two Hebrew letters. (Lévi, Dogme et rituel (1854–56))
