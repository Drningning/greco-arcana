Inner Council · selected author

Albert Pike

1809–1891

Scottish Rite Freemasonry and comparative esoteric philosophy

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

An American lawyer, soldier and writer, Pike served as Sovereign Grand Commander of the Supreme Council of the Scottish Rite, Southern Jurisdiction, from 1859 until his death. He revised the rituals of the Rite and published Morals and Dogma of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite of Freemasonry in 1871.

Contribution to the tradition

Pike's lectures on the degrees range across Hermeticism, Qabalah, Gnosticism and comparative religion, and substantial portions of <cite>Morals and Dogma</cite> draw on Éliphas Lévi. He demonstrates how Freemasonry was read in the nineteenth century as a doorway into the wider Western mysteries.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced · Public domain

Morals and Dogma

Albert Pike

Morals and Dogma of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite of Freemasonry

First published 1871

Where to read
Selected readings: the first three chapters (Apprentice, Fellowcraft, Master). Return later to the chapter on the 28th degree, Knight of the Sun, which surveys the Hermetic and Kabbalistic sources Pike drew upon.
Why it belongs here
Pike shows how nineteenth-century Freemasonry was read as a gateway to Hermeticism, Qabalah and comparative religion. He also shows the student how much of that reading depended on Éliphas Lévi.
Investigate while reading
Where does Pike present history, and where does he present interpretation? Mark the passages that point beyond the Craft toward later stages of this curriculum.
Connections
Read after Mackey and Wilmshurst. Pike's debt to Lévi becomes visible in Stage V; his chapter on the Knight of the Sun anticipates Stages III and V.
