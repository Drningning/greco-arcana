Outer Council · selected author

Proclus

412–485

Neoplatonic metaphysics

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

Born in Constantinople, Proclus headed the Platonic school at Athens for nearly fifty years. His works include the Elements of Theology, the Platonic Theology and a commentary on the Timaeus.

Contribution to the tradition

Proclus gave Neoplatonism its most systematic form, setting out procession, remaining and return as a sequence of propositions. Through Christian and Arabic intermediaries his metaphysics reached the medieval and Renaissance thinkers of Stages III and V.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced

The Elements of Theology

Proclus

Written in the 5th century; translated by E. R. Dodds (1933)

Where to read
Read the opening propositions on unity and multiplicity, then the propositions on procession and reversion (25–39).
Why it belongs here
Proclus states the Neoplatonic system as a sequence of propositions, including the triad of remaining, procession and return that Diagram 6 illustrates.
Investigate while reading
Restate propositions 25–39 in your own words. What does it mean for an effect to remain in its cause?
Connections
Systematizes Plotinus and Iamblichus. Underlies the emanation of the Sephiroth studied in Stage V.
