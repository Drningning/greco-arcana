John Dee

1527–1608/9

Mathematics, Hermetic philosophy and angelic magic

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

An English mathematician, astrologer and adviser to Elizabeth I, Dee published the Monas Hieroglyphica in 1564 and the influential preface to the first English Euclid in 1570. From 1582 he recorded conversations with angels conducted through the scryer Edward Kelley.

Contribution to the tradition

Dee's angelic records, published in part by Meric Casaubon in 1659, are the source of the Enochian system later adapted by the Golden Dawn. He embodies the Renaissance conviction that mathematics, philosophy and theurgy belong to one inquiry.

A True and Faithful Relation

John Dee

A True and Faithful Relation of What Passed for Many Yeers Between Dr. John Dee ... and Some Spirits

Edited by Meric Casaubon, London, 1659

Why it belongs here
The record of Dee and Kelley's angelic sessions, published to discredit Dee, preserved the material from which the Enochian system was later reconstructed.
Investigate while reading
Read a single session closely. How were questions put, and how were the answers recorded?
Connections
Dee was a reader of Agrippa (Stage III). The Golden Dawn adapted the Enochian material into its system.
