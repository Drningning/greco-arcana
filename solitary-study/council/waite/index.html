Inner Council · selected author

Arthur Edward Waite

1857–1942

Christian mysticism, Rosicrucian history and the Tarot

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

Born in Brooklyn and raised in England, Waite joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1891 and later led his own Independent and Rectified Rite. He designed the Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot with the artist Pamela Colman Smith (published by Rider in 1909) and wrote widely on Freemasonry, the Grail and the Rosicrucians.

Contribution to the tradition

Waite is both a historian of the Rosicrucian current and the designer of the most influential occult Tarot. His scholarship is cautious about extravagant claims of lineage, which makes him an essential corrective throughout the curriculum.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Introductory · Public domain

The Pictorial Key to the Tarot

Arthur Edward Waite

Rider, 1910 (illustrated edition dated 1911)

Where to read
Part II (the doctrine behind the veil) for the trumps; Part III for the minor arcana and methods of reading.
Why it belongs here
Waite's guide to the deck he designed with Pamela Colman Smith. He is deliberately reticent about the Golden Dawn correspondences behind it, which is itself a lesson.
Investigate while reading
Where does Waite hint at correspondences he chooses not to state?

The Brotherhood of the Rosy Cross

Arthur Edward Waite

The Brotherhood of the Rosy Cross: Being Records of the House of the Holy Spirit in Its Inward and Outward History

First published 1924

Why it belongs here
Waite's history separates the documentary record of the Rosicrucian movement from later legend. It is a model of how to study an esoteric current critically.
Investigate while reading
Where does Waite find evidence for an actual fraternity, and where does he find only literature?

The Real History of the Rosicrucians

Arthur Edward Waite

First published 1887

Where to read
Contains complete translations of the Fama, the Confessio and the Chymical Wedding.

Waite's earlier study, useful chiefly as the most accessible public-domain source for the three manifestos.

Essential reading · Advanced · Public domain

Transcendental Magic

Éliphas Lévi

Transcendental Magic: Its Doctrine and Ritual (Dogme et rituel de la haute magie)

French original 1854–1856; English translation by A. E. Waite 1896

Where to read
Part I (the Doctrine) and Part II (the Ritual), each in twenty-two chapters keyed to the trumps of the Tarot. In Stage VIII, return to the chapters on the Tarot.
Why it belongs here
Lévi joined Qabalah, ceremonial magic and the Tarot into a single philosophy, and the modern occult revival begins with him.
Investigate while reading
How does Lévi use the Hebrew letters to organize his book? What does he mean by the Astral Light?
Connections
The Golden Dawn revised Lévi's attributions of the trumps to the letters (Stage VIII).
