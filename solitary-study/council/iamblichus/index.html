Outer Council · selected author

Iamblichus

c. 245–c. 325

Neoplatonic theurgy

Inclusion in the Council reflects Greco Arcana's recognition of an author's published contribution to the tradition. It does not imply membership, participation, affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship or approval of Greco Arcana, Joseph Greco or The Solitary Study. About the Council

Biographical overview

A Syrian Neoplatonist who studied in the circle of Porphyry, Iamblichus founded his own school, probably at Apamea. On the Mysteries defends theurgy against Porphyry's objections.

Contribution to the tradition

Iamblichus argued that the soul is restored to the divine not by thought alone but through ritual acts that the gods themselves established. That argument is the philosophical root of theurgy in the Western tradition.

Most significant work in this curriculum

Essential reading · Advanced · Public domain

On the Mysteries

Iamblichus

On the Mysteries (De mysteriis)

Written c. 300; Thomas Taylor's English translation 1821

Why it belongs here
Iamblichus defends theurgy, divine work performed through ritual, against the purely contemplative path. This is the philosophical root of every later claim that ritual can change the soul.
Investigate while reading
Why does Iamblichus insist that the gods, not human thought, make theurgy effective? How does this differ from Plotinus?
Connections
Responds to the Plotinian tradition through Porphyry. Returns in Stage VII as the philosophical justification of ceremonial practice.

Place in the tradition

Historical influences

  • Plotinus Documented historical influence

    Iamblichus studied in the circle of Porphyry, Plotinus's student and editor, and developed theurgy in debate with that school. (Iamblichus, On the Mysteries)

Subsequent developments

  • Theurgy Documented historical influence

    Iamblichus's On the Mysteries is the classic defense of theurgy. (Iamblichus, On the Mysteries)

  • Proclus Documented historical influence

    Proclus inherited and systematized Iamblichean Neoplatonism, including theurgy. (Proclus, Elements of Theology; Platonic Theology)

  • Marsilio Ficino Textual transmission or adaptation

    Ficino made a Latin paraphrase of On the Mysteries, printed in 1497.
