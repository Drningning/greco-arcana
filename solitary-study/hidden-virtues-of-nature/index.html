Stage IV Natura Arcana

The Hidden Virtues of Nature

How are invisible principles traditionally understood to express themselves through nature?

Recommended duration: about 4 months · 2 essential readings · 4 exercises ·

i. The Threshold

A scholar of fifteenth-century Florence has studied too long. He is pale and sleepless, and his thoughts turn inward and dark. His physician does not tell him to stop studying. He tells him that scholars are especially exposed to Saturn, the planet of contemplation and melancholy, and prescribes the gentle influences of Jupiter, Venus and the Sun: walks in green places, music, pleasant scents, the company of friends, gold and sunlight.

The physician is Marsilio Ficino, and the prescription is drawn, in substance, from his Three Books on Life. To a modern reader it may look like a mixture of sensible advice and superstition. To Ficino it was a single coherent system. The planets, the elements, the human temperaments and the plants and stones of the earth all participate in the same order, so a remedy can work through any of them.

This stage studies that system as its practitioners understood it.

ii. The Purpose

The purpose of this stage is to understand the traditional reasoning behind the choice of materials, symbols and times. You will learn the four elements and their qualities, the seven classical planets and their correspondences, the outlines of traditional astrology including planetary hours and elections, and the logic of the talisman (Figures 10, 11 and 12).

This stage contributes directly to everything that follows. The planetary attributions of the Tree of Life in Stage V, the elemental and planetary symbolism of ceremonial magic in Stage VII and the astrological correspondences of Hermetic Tarot in Stage VIII all assume the knowledge gained here.

It also requires historical care. Learned natural magic, contemporary magical practice and regional folk traditions are different things. Folk magic should not be assumed to descend from Agrippa simply because it uses correspondences.

iii. Prerequisites

  • Stage III, The Science of Correspondence.
  • Agrippa's three worlds, especially the elemental world of Book I.
  • Your observation notes from the last exercise of Stage III.
  • Before beginning, review Stage III, The Science of Correspondence.

iv. The Intellectual Architecture

The essential readings move from medieval natural philosophy to modern practice. Albertus Magnus shows a thirteenth-century philosopher reasoning carefully about the powers of stones. Christopher Warnock explains how traditional astrology was applied to the making of talismans, demonstrating the reasoning that Ficino and Agrippa describe.

The supplementary readings document contemporary and folk practice. Scott Cunningham's encyclopedia collects modern herbal associations; Paul Huson's Mastering Witchcraft shows how older grimoire and folk material was reshaped in the twentieth-century revival; Judika Illes surveys folk magic across many cultures; and Gemma Gary records a regional Cornish tradition with its own logic of place and season. Read them comparatively. Ask where a modern association agrees with the older planetary rulers, where it diverges, and where a tradition has developed independently.

The dependency map (Figure 12) shows why this stage matters for practice. A traditional talisman is the meeting point of several kinds of knowledge: the cosmology that explains why it might work, the astrology that chooses its moment, the natural magic that chooses its material, and the symbolism that chooses its image and name.

Hermetic & Renaissance philosophy

Marsilio Ficino author · Stage III

Ficino reintroduced Plato, Plotinus and Hermes to the Latin West and joined them in one philosophy. His astrological medicine in the third book of <cite>De vita</cite> is the bridge between Platonic cosmology and practical celestial magic.

  • Documented historical influence → Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa: Agrippa draws extensively on Ficino's De vita in the first two books of his Occult Philosophy. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia I–II)
Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa author · Stage III

Agrippa organized the magical knowledge of antiquity and the Middle Ages into three worlds: elemental, celestial and intellectual. His synthesis joins natural magic, astrology, Christian Cabala and ceremonial magic, and almost every later Western system draws on his tables.

  • Documented historical influence → Natural magic: Book I of Agrippa's Occult Philosophy is devoted to natural magic.

Astrology, natural & folk magic

Traditional astrology tradition · Stage IV

The traditional science of the seven planets, the zodiac, the houses and the choice of times.

  • Documented historical influence → Marsilio Ficino: Ficino's astrological medicine applies traditional astrology.
  • Practical application → Talismanic magic: Astrology selects the time and planetary image of a talisman.
Picatrix text · Stage IV

A manual of astrological magic translated from Arabic (Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm) into Spanish and Latin in the thirteenth century; translated into English by Greer and Warnock.

  • Documented historical influence → Marsilio Ficino: Ficino's De vita III draws on the astrological magic of the Picatrix tradition. (Ficino, De vita libri tres III)
Natural magic tradition · Stage IV

The use of the hidden virtues of stones, plants, metals and animals, treated by medieval and Renaissance authors as a branch of natural philosophy.

  • Practical application → Talismanic magic: Natural magic selects the material of a talisman.
  • Comparative symbolic relationship → Folk and traditional magic: Folk practice and learned natural magic share a logic of correspondence; this does not establish a common origin.
Albertus Magnus author · Stage IV

Albertus treated the hidden powers of stones, plants and animals as a subject of natural philosophy. Agrippa cites him, and several later books of secrets circulated under his name.

  • Documented historical influence → Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa: Agrippa cites Albertus on the virtues of natural things. (Agrippa, De occulta philosophia I)
  • Documented historical influence → Natural magic: Albertus treated the virtues of stones and plants as natural philosophy. (Albertus Magnus, De mineralibus)
Talismanic magic concept · Stage IV

The making of images and objects at chosen times to draw on celestial influence, where cosmology, astrology, natural magic and symbolism meet.

Folk and traditional magic tradition · Stage IV

Regional and popular magical practice, with its own logic of place, season and material; not to be assumed to descend from learned magic.

Scott Cunningham author · Stage IV

Cunningham's encyclopedia gathers traditional associations of plants for practical use. It is read here as a reference to contemporary practice, to be compared with the older natural philosophy of Albertus and Agrippa.

  • Practical application → Natural magic: Cunningham's encyclopedia applies traditional plant associations in contemporary practice.
Judika Illes author · Stage IV

Illes documents folk and practical magic from many cultures. Her work shows how widely correspondence-based practice spread, and why folk traditions should not be assumed to descend from learned magic.

  • Practical application → Folk and traditional magic: Illes documents folk and practical magic across many cultures.
Gemma Gary author · Stage IV

Gary records a regional folk tradition with its own logic of place, season and material. It is studied as a living folk practice, not as a descendant of Renaissance occult philosophy.

  • Practical application → Folk and traditional magic: Gary records the folk magic of Cornwall.
Christopher Warnock author · Stage IV

Warnock teaches how traditional astrology was applied to the making of talismans: the selection of image, material and moment. He shows the reasoning behind practices that Ficino and Agrippa describe in theory.

  • Practical application → Talismanic magic: Warnock teaches the traditional practice of astrological talismans.
  • Textual transmission or adaptation → Picatrix: Warnock co-translated the Picatrix into English with John Michael Greer.

Tarot

Paul Huson author · Stage IV

Huson's history of the Tarot traces the cards' imagery to medieval and Renaissance sources before the occult reinterpretations of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, which keeps Stage VIII historically honest.

  • Practical application → Folk and traditional magic: Mastering Witchcraft (1970) reshapes grimoire and folk material for the witchcraft revival.

vi. The Masonic Connection

Documented Astronomy is among the seven liberal arts commended in the historical Fellowcraft lectures, and celestial symbols such as the sun, the moon and the blazing star appear among Masonic emblems.

Greco Arcana interpretation The Craft's working tools are given moral meanings, but in the traditional worldview the materials and times of a work also carried meaning. Reading the lodge's sun, moon and stars with the knowledge of this stage invites the student to consider that the temple is oriented not only in space but in time, a theme the Craft expresses in its attention to the east and to the meridian sun. This is a comparative reading.

vii. The Work

These exercises develop understanding; they promise no supernatural results. Record each in your study journal.

  1. Observation of traditional correspondences

    Build your table of seven

    Using Agrippa and Warnock, make your own table of the seven planets with their day, metal, color, temperament and two plants each. Compare it with Figure 11 and note any sources that disagree.

  2. Observation of traditional correspondences

    Keep the planetary hours

    Learn to calculate the planetary hours for your location. For two weeks, record the planetary hour in which you begin important tasks. Return to your Stage III observations and compare.

  3. Symbolic analysis

    Analyze a talisman

    Choose a traditional planetary talisman described by Agrippa or Warnock. Without making it, list every decision involved: intention, planet, election, material, image, name and consecration. Mark which decisions depend on which kind of knowledge (Figure 12).

  4. Comparative reading

    Folk and learned magic

    Choose one practice from Illes or Gary and one from Agrippa that use similar materials. Write a page on whether they share a logic, a history, both or neither.

viii. Standard of Completion

Advance because you understand the material, not because you have finished reading it.

Learning objectives

  • Name the four elements with their qualities and the seven classical planets with their principal correspondences.
  • Explain planetary hours and the basic principle of electional astrology.
  • Explain the traditional reasoning behind selecting materials, symbols and times for a magical operation.
  • Distinguish historical natural philosophy, contemporary practice and folk tradition.
  • Explain how this stage prepares for ceremonial magic and Hermetic Tarot.

Questions for self-assessment

  1. Could you explain why a traditional talisman of the Sun is made of gold and begun on a Sunday in the hour of the Sun?
  2. What is the difference between the Chaldean order of the planets and the order of the days of the week, and how are they related?
  3. Why should folk magic not be assumed to descend from Agrippa?
  4. Which correspondences in this stage will reappear on the Tree of Life?

ix. The Next Threshold

The elements and planets are now familiar. But how does a single divine source become this manifold world of elements, planets, stones and plants? The Neoplatonists answered with emanation. The Kabbalists answered with a diagram of ten emanations and twenty-two paths, and the Western tradition adopted it as its great organizing map. The next stage studies the Tree of Life.
