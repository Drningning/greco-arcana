The Gaming Board of Gwenddoleu

Set the silver men on the gold squares. Then take your hands away.

By Joseph Greco·24 November 2026·2 min read

The object

One of the Thirteen Treasures of Britain, this board belonged to Gwenddoleu son of Ceidio. The Welsh treasure list describes gold squares and silver playing pieces. Once set on the board, the pieces would play by themselves.

When it vanished

Around 573 CE is the date to circle. The Welsh annals place Gwenddoleu’s death at the Battle of Arfderydd around then. Last suspected region: the border country near Arthuret in present-day Cumbria, where the battle is commonly placed. That puts the owner in the area. The board’s whereabouts that day are unknown.

The clues

The Welsh list gives us the owner, the materials, and the condition that makes it work. Somebody has to place the men on the board. So a board sitting motionless in a chest would tell you very little. And if its silver men had been taken as spoils and passed to someone else, each person could have walked away thinking they’d gotten the less interesting half.

How to pursue it

Follow the owner’s name through the Welsh traditions about Arfderydd, then follow the board through different versions of the treasure list. Some translations call it a chessboard, others a draughtboard. Search for gold squares and silver men. Those details give you a better lead than the name of the game. And if you ever find the set, the old account tells you exactly what to do next.
