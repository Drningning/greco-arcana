The Golden Menorah of Jerusalem

Rome carved it in stone. The gold is another matter.

The object

The seven-branched golden lampstand carried out of Jerusalem’s Second Temple after its destruction in 70 CE. Josephus describes it in the Roman triumph, and the relief on the Arch of Titus still shows it being borne through Rome. That gives us two things a treasure hunter rarely gets together: a written account and an ancient image of the same object in transit.

When it vanished

Its fate becomes uncertain in late antiquity. The sack of Rome in 455 CE is the date to circle if you follow the Vandal trail. Last specifically named location: Vespasian’s Temple of Peace in Rome, where Josephus says the Temple spoils were deposited.

The clues

Centuries later, Procopius tells of Jewish treasures taken by the Vandals to Carthage, then carried to Constantinople by the victorious Byzantines. He says Justinian sent them on to Christian sanctuaries in Jerusalem. That gives the hunt a remarkable possible route back to the city where it began. It also leaves one word missing from the account of the later journey: menorah.

How to pursue it

Read Josephus and Procopius side by side. Underline every object each man actually names. The decisive find would be a later inventory or description that singles out a golden, seven-branched lampstand among the spoils. Until that turns up, Rome is the last firm lead. Jerusalem is the most intriguing one.
