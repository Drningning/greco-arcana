§ I

The artifact

{{figure:walters|right}}

Of all the treasures attributed to King Solomon, perhaps none was more valuable than a single ring.

According to the Testament of Solomon, it was delivered to the king by the Archangel Michael. With it, Solomon was able to compel supernatural beings to participate in the construction of his Temple.

It was not merely an instrument of protection. It was an instrument of authority.

Its whereabouts following Solomon's reign remain a mystery.

§ II

The last known whereabouts

{{figure:temple|wide|Reconstruction of Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem.}}

The surviving legends place the ring in ancient Jerusalem during the construction of the First Temple.

Although the biblical account describes Solomon's successor, Rehoboam, inheriting the kingdom, no reliable tradition establishes that he inherited the ring.

Was it buried with Solomon?

Concealed among the treasures of the Temple?

Returned to its original giver?

The surviving sources do not resolve these questions.

§ III · Evidence assembled

The clues

ILiterary testimony

Clue I: It was bestowed, not manufactured

The Testament of Solomon describes the ring as a divine gift delivered through Michael.

If the account is taken literally, the original ring cannot simply be reproduced by a skilled goldsmith. Its origin may be as important as its inscription.

Source: Testament of Solomon, chapter 1

IILiterary testimony

Clue II: The inscription

The Babylonian Talmud describes a ring engraved with the divine name.

Other traditions associate Solomon's seal with the pentagram or hexagram. A six-pointed star alone, however, would be insufficient to establish the authenticity of an object claiming to be the original ring.

Source: Babylonian Talmud, Gittin 68a–68b

IIILiterary testimony

Clue III: It has been lost before

According to the Talmud, the demon Ashmedai persuaded Solomon to surrender the ring before overthrowing him.

Solomon subsequently recovered his authority. The legend establishes that the ring could be separated from its rightful possessor.

Source: Babylonian Talmud, Gittin 68a–68b

IVHistorical tradition

Clue IV: The sea returns what it claims

In another tradition, recorded by the historian al-Tabari, the ring is cast into the sea, swallowed by a fish and eventually recovered.

Curiously, Solomon recovers the source of his authority after losing his position as king.

Source: al-Tabari's historical writings

VScriptural

Clue V: Wisdom preceded possession

In the biblical narrative, when Solomon was offered a gift from God, he requested wisdom rather than wealth, longevity or the destruction of his enemies.

It is worth remembering that Solomon was renowned for his wisdom before the magical traditions placed the ring upon his finger.

Source: 1 Kings 3

§ IV

The path to attainment

Here is where the accounts become particularly interesting to a student of the Western Mystery Tradition.

The legends suggest three potential conditions surrounding possession of the ring.

  • Wisdom Solomon sought understanding before acquiring supernatural authority.
  • Purpose The ring was bestowed in connection with the construction of a sacred edifice, rather than the accumulation of personal wealth.
  • Humility In the legends of its loss and recovery, Solomon experiences the deprivation of his earthly authority.

These are not documented instructions for finding the physical artifact. They are clues that emerge from comparing the stories surrounding its possession.

Like Perceval approaching the Holy Grail, perhaps the seeker must satisfy certain conditions before the object can be attained.

Perhaps finding it and being worthy of possessing it are two entirely different accomplishments.

§ V · Attributed to Joseph Greco

The practitioner's notes

There is a curious distinction between possessing a magical instrument and possessing the authority that makes it magical.

If the power resides entirely within the ring, then any thief who acquires it becomes a magician.

But if the ring is the physical expression of an authority already established within its wearer, then the object itself may be only half of the treasure.

Consider that Solomon was first given wisdom, and later, according to magical tradition, given a means by which to exercise extraordinary authority.

Are we searching for the ring, or are we searching for that which made Solomon worthy of receiving it?

The physical hunt remains open.

So does the other.
